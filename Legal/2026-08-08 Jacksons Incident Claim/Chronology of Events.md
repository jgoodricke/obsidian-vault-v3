## 15th June 2026
On 15 June 2026 at approximately 10:36 am, Jackson was participating in an outdoor wooden balancing-log activity at Maples Early Learning Hawthorn East.

According to Maples’ account following its CCTV review:
- An educator, Lexi, was supervising the activity and assisting the child immediately ahead of Jackson.
- Jackson stepped forward before that child had finished crossing, lost his balance, fell between the logs and landed face-first on a wooden log.
- The incident report states that the child ahead of Jackson also fell.
- Lexi was beside Jackson but was unable to catch him because she was assisting the other child.

### Injury and immediate symptoms
The impact caused Jackson’s tooth to penetrate the inside of his left cheek, resulting in a deep internal mouth laceration and bleeding. The incident report photographs also show visible redness, bruising or swelling around his left cheek and mouth.

Maples stated that Jackson:
- did not initially cry and had no immediately visible bleeding;
- began bleeding after he was picked up;
- appeared unusually quiet and unbalanced; and
- was monitored for possible signs of concussion.

### First aid and collection
The original incident report records compression or pressure, water and contacting Jackson’s parents. Maples later stated that staff also:
- applied an ice pack;
- visually assessed the injury;
- continuously monitored, comforted and supported Jackson; and
- changed his clothing because it had blood on it.

James was contacted at 10:34 am (according to his phone records) and collected Jackson at 10:53 am.

James asked if it was serious enough for him to go to Hospital, and was told by the educators there that they did not know. As such, James said that he would take Jackson to  his GP as soon as possible. No ambulance attended.

### Medical Treatment
James left Maples Early Learning at 11:10, and proceeded to the GP clinic. He arrived at the GP at 11:48 am. The GP attended to the injury but said that he was unable to treat it, and James took Jackson to the Royal Children's Hospital at 1:04. He was seen by a resident doctor but he did not recieve surgery, so James, Jackson and Christy left the hospital at 5:19 pm.

## 15th June 2026
### Medical treatment and recovery
Jackson underwent surgery on 16 June 2026, the day after the incident, and received stitches. His parents subsequently advised Maples that:
- he required a soft-food diet for approximately three weeks;
- he had a follow-up appointment with the surgeon;
- he was expected to be medically unable to attend childcare for about three weeks; and
- he was later assessed by a plastic surgeon and required ongoing scar treatment and medical management.

### Maples’ subsequent response

Maples stated that it reviewed the CCTV, spoke with the educators, held a team meeting and completed a risk assessment. Although it maintained that the balancing logs were appropriate for Jackson’s age group, it decided to lower the logs and place cut log halves in the gaps between them.

The incident was notified to the Department of Education on 16 June 2026 under reference **NOT-00310277**. Maples later advised that the Department closed the notification on 24 July 2026.

### Details that are not fully reconciled from the 16th and 17th

The records contain several differences or omissions:
- The incident report lists the witness as “None”, although Maples later said Lexi was beside Jackson and supervising the activity.
- Maples’ first email referred to Jackson playing with “three other children”, while its later response said there were three children in total, including Jackson.
- The report marked the event as not being a serious incident and recorded that medical attention was not sought, despite Jackson being taken for medical assessment and undergoing surgery the following day.
- Maples later said the medical-attention entry was intended to mean that emergency services did not attend, although the form contains separate questions for emergency services and medical attention.
- The original report recorded only compression and water as first aid. The ice pack, clothing change and more extensive monitoring were described later by Maples.
- Maples preserved the CCTV footage but declined to provide access, so its description of the footage has not been independently verified by the family.
