## Summary of the dispute

I seek assistance to resolve responsibility for the reported cracking, uneven floor levels, inadequate surface drainage, moisture concerns, and the other building items listed below. I seek appropriate investigation and rectification of any defective or incomplete work for which the builder is responsible.

I notified the builder on 11 June 2026 and supplied the building inspection report. The builder inspected on 2 July 2026. It subsequently denied responsibility for the drainage and cracking and declined further investigation or rectification of those issues. For some other items, it offered inspection or possible work, as recorded below. The supplied correspondence does not confirm completion of that work.

The central dispute is whether the conditions arise from original building or site works, later landscaping or other external works, maintenance, normal movement, or a combination of causes. I do not accept that later landscaping has been established as the sole cause. Equally, I do not claim that the reports establish structural failure or prove that every listed item is a construction defect.

The later engineering report provides further evidence and recommendations. It also qualifies some concerns in the earlier building report. In particular, it describes the observed wall cracking as category 0 or 1 and non-structural, and says that the garage slab cracking does not threaten structural integrity. These findings do not resolve the separate questions about floor levels, drainage, causation, and responsibility for the recommended work.

## A. Cracking, floor levels, drainage and bathroom moisture

### 1. Garage concrete slab cracking and localised dip

- **Location:** Garage floor slab.
- **Matter in dispute:** The building report records significant cracking and recommends engineering assessment. The engineering report records cracks from hairline to approximately 2 mm, together with a localised dip and discolouration. It identifies differential footing movement and normal concrete shrinkage as possible causes. It says the cracks do not pose a structural integrity risk and do not require repair, although filling is an option. It recommends a concrete overlay to raise the low area. Whether the slab condition involves defective building work, and who is responsible for any required work, remain disputed.
- **Builder's position:** Denies a defect attributable to its work and attributes movement and cracking to drainage, landscaping and post-handover conditions. Its July response does not provide a detailed garage crack survey or slab-level assessment.
- **Outcome requested:** Assess the cracking and dip together with matter 4. Agree on any further investigation, monitoring, and work needed to restore floor serviceability. Rectify any condition attributable to defective builder work. I do not seek slab replacement or structural repair without supporting engineering advice.
- **Evidence:** B, major defect 1.01, pp. 12–13; E, observation 4, pp. 17–18, and floor survey, pp. 9–11; C, pp. 2–3 and 9–11.

### 2. External brickwork cracking

- **Location:** External brickwork, including above the garage entrance and along the western external wall.
- **Matter in dispute:** The building report describes category 2 cracking. The later engineering report records stepped and vertical mortar-joint cracks up to approximately 1 mm above the garage entrance and less than 1 mm along the western wall, classified as category 1. It does not identify a current structural risk from those cracks. The cause, any progression, and responsibility for investigation and repair remain unresolved.
- **Builder's position:** Attributes the cracking to reactive soil movement caused by post-construction drainage and landscaping changes, rather than defective building work.
- **Outcome requested:** Record and assess the cracks, including the difference between the reports' classifications. Investigate the underlying movement with matters 3 and 4. Agree on monitoring and appropriate masonry repairs after moisture conditions have stabilised, where repair is required and the builder is responsible. Underpinning is not sought without further engineering justification.
- **Evidence:** B, major defect 1.02, pp. 14–15; E, observations 5 and 8, pp. 19 and 24; C, pp. 3 and 9–11.

### 3. Inadequate surface drainage, ponding and disputed ground levels

- **Location:** Ground and external surfaces around the dwelling and slab perimeter.
- **Matter in dispute:** The building report identifies improperly graded gravel and water pooling against the house. The engineer also records signs of inadequate perimeter drainage and recommends a designed drainage solution. The builder acknowledges inadequate falls and ponding, but says later landscaping, garden edging and paving altered the original grades. The supplied correspondence does not include the original level records, handover photographs or supporting measurements requested to establish those changes and their effect.
- **Builder's position:** Its 13 July email records "inadequate falls away from the slab perimeter" and "water ponding at the edge of the slab". It nevertheless says the original work complied at completion, that it did not undertake landscaping, and that the owner or those responsible for later works must rectify the drainage. It proposes no builder drainage works.
- **Outcome requested:** Assess present levels, falls, runoff paths and discharge arrangements against the approved documents and applicable requirements. Review construction and handover records to distinguish original works from later changes and assess their contribution. Agree on temporary measures to reduce further water damage and a properly designed permanent solution, including responsibility, scope and timing. The engineer recommends suitable subgrade preparation and avoiding adverse effects on neighbouring properties.
- **Evidence:** B, major defect 1.03, p. 16; E, observation 6, pp. 20–21, and recommendations, pp. 6 and 10–11; C, pp. 5, 7 and 9–11.

### 4. Uneven floor levels and adequacy of the footing system

- **Location:** Dwelling and garage floor planes; underlying slab, footings and founding material.
- **Matter in dispute:** The engineer measured a maximum floor-level differential of 54 mm within the dwelling and 34 mm within the garage, assessed as separate floor planes. The report considers differential footing movement likely, but warns that one survey does not establish total historical movement or attribute every level difference to movement. It identifies Class P site conditions, evidence of pre-construction filling, and drawings providing for bored piers through fill into natural material. It recommends further investigation and records review, rather than confirming a construction failure. Responsibility for that investigation and any resulting work is disputed.
- **Builder's position:** Says its inspection found no structural defect attributable to its work, declines further investigation, and says the owner must obtain and pay for an independent engineering assessment. The supplied correspondence does not address the later measured floor levels.
- **Outcome requested:** Review the original geotechnical report, approved architectural and structural plans, permit and inspection records, and available footing construction records. Arrange the recommended stormwater and sewer investigation, including CCTV and pressure testing where possible, and gutter/downpipe checks. Obtain an engineering assessment of the footing system as designed and constructed. If cracking persists after moisture issues are rectified, undertake the recommended geotechnical investigation near the garage. Agree on responsibility for investigation and any supported rectification. No plumbing leak, missing pier or footing failure is asserted as an established fact.
- **Evidence:** E, executive summary, p. 6; observation 1, pp. 9–11; observation 9, p. 25; structural drawings, pp. 36–39; C, pp. 9–11.

## Resolution sought

I seek a written agreement that:

1. Identifies the matters suitable for BPC assessment and conciliation, including any further technical investigation needed to resolve causation and responsibility.
2. Records responsibility for each item, the agreed investigation or rectification scope, who will carry it out, responsibility for costs, and completion dates.
3. Addresses moisture and drainage causes before permanent crack or finish repairs. Where plumbing defects allowing moisture into soil around the footings are identified and rectified, allows the minimum six-month dry-off period recommended in engineering report pp. 10–11 before relevant repairs. For other moisture conditions, obtains engineering advice on the required stabilisation period and repair timing.
4. Provides for suitable verification of completed work and monitoring of floor levels and cracking where recommended.

I remain willing to provide reasonable access for properly arranged inspections and testing. Any intrusive investigation or permanent work should first have a written scope agreed with me. Reasonable temporary measures to reduce further water-related damage should not be delayed unnecessarily.

The numbered items identify the requested assessment scope; they are not a claim that every reported condition is a proven defect. Compliance should be assessed against the approved documents and requirements applicable when the relevant work was carried out. References in the reports to later NCC or standards editions are not, by themselves, proof of non-compliance in the original work.
