- **BPC / DBDRV reference:** DBDRV-2026-08-54054

- **Builder:** Symmetric Homes Pty Ltd, as named in the building permit
- **Purpose:** Response to the request for information dated 21 October 2026.

## Summary of the dispute

The dispute concerns responsibility for the reported cracking, uneven floor levels, inadequate surface drainage, moisture concerns, and the other building items listed below. I seek BPC assistance to independently assess the disputed building conditions and determine whether defective or incomplete work attributable to the builder is present. Where builder responsibility is established, I seek appropriate rectification addressing the underlying causes, not merely the visible symptoms. Further technical investigation should be undertaken where necessary to determine causation, responsibility or the appropriate repair scope.

I notified the builder on 11 June 2026 and supplied the building inspection report. The builder inspected on 2 July 2026. It subsequently denied responsibility for the drainage and cracking and declined further investigation or rectification of those issues. For some other items, it offered inspection or possible work, as recorded below. The supplied correspondence does not confirm completion of that work.

The central dispute is whether the conditions arise from original building or site works, later landscaping or other external works, maintenance, normal movement, or a combination of causes. I do not accept that later landscaping has been established as the sole cause. Equally, I do not claim that the reports establish structural failure or prove that every listed item is a construction defect.

The later engineering report provides further evidence and recommendations. It also qualifies some concerns in the earlier building report. In particular, it describes the observed wall cracking as category 0 or 1 and non-structural, and says that the garage slab cracking does not threaten structural integrity. These findings do not resolve the separate questions about floor levels, drainage, causation, and responsibility for the recommended work.

## A. Cracking, floor levels, drainage and bathroom moisture

### 1. Garage concrete slab cracking and localised dip

- **Location:** Garage floor slab.
- **Matter in dispute:** The building report records significant cracking and recommends engineering assessment. The engineering report records cracks from hairline to approximately 2 mm, together with a localised dip and discolouration. It identifies differential footing movement and normal concrete shrinkage as possible causes. It says the cracks do not pose a structural integrity risk and do not require repair, although filling is an option. It recommends a concrete overlay to raise the low area. Whether the slab condition involves defective building work, and who is responsible for any required work, remain disputed.
- **Builder's position:** Denies a defect attributable to its work and attributes movement and cracking to drainage, landscaping and post-handover conditions. Its July response does not provide a detailed garage crack survey or slab-level assessment.
- **Outcome requested:** 
	- Request an independent assessment of the garage slab cracking and localised dip, including their possible relationship to footing movement and drainage. Determine whether the slab condition constitutes defective building work or adversely affects its intended use. 
	- Where rectification is warranted and attributable to the builder, seek appropriately specified repairs addressing the underlying cause and restoring serviceability. 
	- Permanent filling or overlay works should not proceed before the relevant movement and drainage concerns have been assessed.
- **Evidence:** B, major defect 1.01, pp. 12–13; E, observation 4, pp. 17–18, and floor survey, pp. 9–11; C, pp. 2–3 and 9–11.

### 2. External brickwork cracking

- **Location:** External brickwork, including above the garage entrance and along the western external wall.
- **Matter in dispute:** The building report describes category 2 cracking. The later engineering report records stepped and vertical mortar-joint cracks up to approximately 1 mm above the garage entrance and less than 1 mm along the western wall, classified as category 1. It does not identify a current structural risk from those cracks. The cause, any progression, and responsibility for investigation and repair remain unresolved.
- **Builder's position:** Attributes the cracking to reactive soil movement caused by post-construction drainage and landscaping changes, rather than defective building work.
- **Outcome requested:** 
	- Request an independent assessment of the external brickwork cracking, taking into account the findings of both reports and its possible relationship to the floor-level and drainage concerns. 
	- Where defective builder work is established, seek appropriate rectification, including any necessary underlying remedial measures and subsequent masonry repairs once movement and relevant moisture conditions have stabilised. 
	- Establish suitable monitoring where recommended.
- **Evidence:** B, major defect 1.02, pp. 14–15; E, observations 5 and 8, pp. 19 and 24; C, pp. 3 and 9–11.

### 3. Inadequate surface drainage, ponding and disputed ground levels

- **Location:** Ground and external surfaces around the dwelling and slab perimeter.
- **Matter in dispute:** The building report identifies improperly graded gravel and water pooling against the house. The engineer also records signs of inadequate perimeter drainage and recommends a designed drainage solution. The builder acknowledges inadequate falls and ponding, but says later landscaping, garden edging and paving altered the original grades. The supplied correspondence does not include the original level records, handover photographs or supporting measurements requested to establish those changes and their effect.
- **Builder's position:** Its 13 July email records "inadequate falls away from the slab perimeter" and "water ponding at the edge of the slab". It nevertheless says the original work complied at completion, that it did not undertake landscaping, and that the owner or those responsible for later works must rectify the drainage. It proposes no builder drainage works.
- **Outcome requested:** 
	- Request an independent assessment of whether the original site works, ground levels and drainage arrangements complied with the applicable requirements and approved documentation, including consideration of any subsequent changes. 
	- Where defective or incomplete work attributable to the builder is identified, seek rectification through an appropriately designed drainage solution that prevents water ponding against or being directed towards the dwelling. 
	- Seek timely temporary measures to mitigate further moisture-related damage while responsibility and permanent rectification are determined.
- **Evidence:** B, major defect 1.03, p. 16; E, observation 6, pp. 20–21, and recommendations, pp. 6 and 10–11; C, pp. 5, 7 and 9–11.

### 4. Uneven floor levels, footing performance and associated internal cracking or separation

- **Location:** Dwelling and garage floor planes; underlying slab, footings and founding material; internal cornice/ceiling junctions, entrance corridor bulkhead, Bedroom 2 plaster near the window and ceiling ventilation unit, and internal skirting-to-wall junctions.
- **Matter in dispute:** The engineer measured maximum floor-level differentials of 54 mm within the dwelling and 34 mm within the garage, assessed as separate floor planes. The report considers differential footing movement likely, but warns that one survey does not establish total historical movement or attribute every level difference to movement. It identifies Class P site conditions, evidence of pre-construction filling, and drawings providing for bored piers through fill into natural material. The building report also records cornice/ceiling-junction cracks, Bedroom 2 plaster cracks and skirting separation. These are included as symptoms to assess with the movement investigation, not as separate proven workmanship defects. The engineer classifies its observed internal cracks as category 0 or 1 and non-structural. Whether the internal conditions relate to footing movement, installation, normal material changes or other causes remains unresolved. Further investigation is recommended, rather than a construction failure being confirmed.
- **Builder's position:** Says its inspection found no structural defect attributable to its work, declines further investigation, and says the owner must obtain and pay for an independent engineering assessment. It treats cornice cracking and skirting separation as non-structural or cosmetic and attributes Bedroom 2 cracking to owner-responsible drainage. These positions do not resolve causation or responsibility. The supplied correspondence does not address the later measured floor levels.
- **Outcome requested:** 
	- Request an independent assessment of the observed floor-level variations and potential footing movement to determine whether they indicate defective design, construction or performance attributable to the builder.
	- Seek a staged investigation informed by the engineering report, including review of the approved structural and geotechnical documentation, relevant permit amendments and available construction and inspection records; assessment of the stormwater and sewer systems, including CCTV and pressure testing where appropriate; and further geotechnical investigation if subsequent findings justify it.
	- Where defective builder work is established, seek rectification addressing the underlying cause and any associated damage, together with appropriate monitoring and verification. Responsibility for necessary investigations, costs, repairs and completion timeframes should be expressly addressed.
- **Evidence:** B, minor defects 2.08, 2.11 and 2.13, pp. 24, 27 and 28; E, executive summary, p. 6; observation 1, pp. 9–11; observation 2, pp. 12–14; observation 9, p. 25; structural drawings, pp. 36–39; C, p. 3, response items 11, 14 and 16, and pp. 9–11; P, p. 1.

### 5. Bathroom shower moisture and possible leakage

- **Location:** Bathroom, at the base of the shower screen and surrounding materials shown in the building report.
- **Matter in dispute:** The building report records elevated moisture readings and evidence of moisture intrusion at the shower-screen base. It recommends evaluation and rectification of sealing or waterproofing. The source and extent of moisture, and whether there is defective sealing, waterproofing, plumbing or associated work, have not been established by the supplied documents.
- **Builder's position:** The supplied correspondence does not give a specific finding or rectification proposal for this item. The July response discusses moisture generally but does not resolve the bathroom concern.
- **Outcome requested:** 
	- Arrange suitable moisture and leak testing to identify the source and affected materials. Rectify any defective builder work and resulting damage for which the builder is responsible, then verify that the leak or moisture issue has been resolved.
	- Request Independent assessment of the reported moisture intrusion and determination of responsibility. Seek appropriately qualified moisture and leak investigation sufficient to identify the source and affected materials, followed by rectification of any defective builder work and resulting damage for which the builder is responsible. 
	- Repairs should address the source of moisture rather than merely conceal visible symptoms, with suitable verification following completion.
- **Evidence:** B, major defect 1.04, p. 17; C, original notification, p. 1, and request to confirm inspection scope, p. 11.

## Resolution sought

I seek a written agreement that:

1. Identifies the matters suitable for BPC assessment and conciliation, including any further technical investigation needed to resolve causation and responsibility.
2. Records responsibility for each item, the agreed investigation or rectification scope, who will carry it out, responsibility for costs, and completion dates.
3. Addresses moisture and drainage causes before permanent crack or finish repairs that depend on their resolution. Where plumbing defects allowing moisture into soil around the footings are identified and rectified, allows the minimum six-month dry-off period recommended in engineering report pp. 10–11 before relevant movement-dependent repairs. For other moisture conditions, obtains engineering advice on the required stabilisation period and repair timing. This is not a blanket delay: inspection and repairs that do not depend on moisture or structural movement stabilising, including securing loose gutter clips, installing a required laundry door seal and resolving bathroom moisture or leakage, should progress within reasonable timeframes.
4. Provides for suitable verification of completed work and monitoring of floor levels and cracking where recommended.

Given the builder's previous inspection and subsequent denial of responsibility for the principal cracking and drainage concerns, I request that disputed findings of defect, causation and responsibility not be determined solely by the builder's own assessment. I seek BPC assessment where appropriate, with further investigation by suitably qualified and independent professionals where necessary. I remain willing to provide reasonable access to the builder and its contractors for inspections and properly scoped works.

Any intrusive investigation or permanent work should first have a written scope agreed with me. Reasonable temporary measures to reduce further water-related damage should not be delayed unnecessarily.

The numbered items identify the matters I ask BPC to consider at this stage; they are not a claim that every reported condition is a proven defect. Other observations in the attached reports are not separately raised in this list. Compliance should be assessed against the approved documents and requirements applicable when the relevant work was carried out. The building and occupancy permits identify BCA 2019; the applicable amendments and approved design should be confirmed. References in the reports to later NCC or standards editions are not, by themselves, proof of non-compliance in the original work.
