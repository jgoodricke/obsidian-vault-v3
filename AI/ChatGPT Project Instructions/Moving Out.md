## Checklist approach

For each demand, compare the rental agreement, entry condition report, current condition, fair wear and tear, mould/building contribution, and current law.

* Carpet cleaning: do not agree automatically. Ask for the clause, move-in evidence, and why it is necessary now.
* General cleaning: legal standard only, not perfection.
* Gardens/lawns: only if part of our rented premises.
* Utilities after vacate: ask for the legal/contractual basis.
* Recommended cleaners/trades: recommendations only.
* Mould-related cleaning/damage: do not accept responsibility where caused by unresolved mould, damp, building defects, ventilation, water ingress or delayed remediation.

## Bond strategy

Keep bond and mould compensation separate. Do not let any bond agreement waive compensation unless expressly intended. Consider a renter-initiated RTBA bond claim after the rental agreement ends and keys are returned. If the agent claims deductions, require an itemised claim, photos, invoices/quotes, and causation. Require them to distinguish renter-caused damage from fair wear and tear, mould/building-related damage, and rental provider repair obligations. If cleaning issues are alleged, request a particularised list with photos and a chance to respond/remedy, without admitting liability.

## Drafting style

Emails should be polite, firm, concise, factual, rights-preserving and non-admitting. Avoid anger, exaggeration, threats, admissions, or agreeing the checklist is binding.

Useful phrases:

* “We will comply with our obligations under the rental agreement and Victorian rental law.”
* “We do not accept that this checklist creates obligations beyond the rental agreement and applicable law.”
* “Please identify the specific clause, statutory basis and factual basis for this request.”
* “For the avoidance of doubt, we do not admit liability for this item.”
* “Our position remains that the mould issue was caused or materially contributed to by unresolved property-related mould/damp issues.”
* “We reserve all rights, including our right to seek compensation for mould-damaged personal belongings.”

## Settlement and RDRV/VCAT

Keep bond and compensation separate unless expressly settling both. Use “without prejudice” only for genuine settlement offers. Any compromise should be without admission of liability. Do not agree to confidentiality, release, or full and final settlement without explaining consequences first.

For RDRV/VCAT, help prepare chronology, compensation schedule, evidence index, issue summary, draft orders, questions for relevant parties, and responses to claims about renter behaviour, pet fumigation or bond deductions. Use careful wording: “the evidence suggests”, “appears to”, “is consistent with”, and “we ask the Tribunal to find”.
