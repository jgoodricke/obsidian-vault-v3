I have been married for 4 years. My wife and I have had basically no sex during the marriage. I have tried talking with her about it many times, but she does not seem to care. After months, she agreed to have sex, but it felt awful because she said beforehand she would not enjoy it, and I felt like she deliberately made sure she did not enjoy it to prove a point.

She often seems to withdraw sex as punishment for perceived slights. If I do something that annoys her, it can mean “no sex” for another two weeks, which effectively means we almost never have sex. Recently I asked if we could have sex, and she became aggressive and said she does not think we will ever have sex again.

I am very tired of the constant rejection, drama, and lack of intimacy. I do not want to pressure her into sex, and I understand sex must be freely wanted by both people. But I also do not want to pretend I am okay with a marriage that has no intimacy, affection, or repair.

Divorce does not feel like an option right now because we have a young son, and I am afraid of being separated from him or dragged into a messy co-parenting situation. I grew up around messy divorces and know how badly things can go when one parent wants to make the other parent’s life difficult. For now, I would rather maintain a strong daily relationship with my son, even if my relationship with my wife is terrible.

The advice so far was:

1. Stop initiating sex for now, because my wife has clearly said she does not want it. This is not about accepting the situation forever, but about not chasing sex from someone who is refusing it.
2. Tell her clearly that I accept she does not want sex, but that the situation is hurting me badly and I need to make changes for my own wellbeing.
3. Suggest sleeping in separate beds for the foreseeable future, framed as a mental health and sleep boundary, not as punishment. Suggested wording:
    
    “I want to sleep separately for the foreseeable future. Sleeping beside you while our relationship is this painful is making me feel worse, and I need some space so I can function better. I’m not doing this to punish you. I’m doing it because I need emotional distance and better sleep.”
    
4. Create distance in a structured way, not a cruel or passive-aggressive way. Be polite, stop seeking emotional reassurance from her, avoid repeated arguments about sex, keep parenting communication practical, and focus more on my own health, friendships, hobbies, counselling, work, and time with my son.
5. Consider asking, at a calmer time, what she believes the marriage now is. Suggested wording:
    
    “I’m not asking you to have sex you don’t want. But I need to know whether you want a romantic marriage with me at all. If the answer is no, then we need to talk honestly about what this marriage is, because I can’t live indefinitely in a relationship where intimacy, affection, and repair are permanently off the table.”
    
6. Open relationships are only ethical and realistic if both people genuinely consent. If my wife does not agree, then it is not an open relationship. It would be cheating or an affair.
7. A possible fair question to ask her later is:
    
    “I accept that you don’t want sex with me, and I’m not going to pressure you. But I need to know whether you still expect sexual exclusivity from me if you are also saying our sexual relationship is over.”
    
8. Secret affairs are risky and likely to create the exact kind of chaos I am afraid of: retaliation, humiliation, conflict, and my son being caught in the middle.
9. Sex work in Victoria was discussed as legally more realistic than I may have assumed, but still emotionally and relationally risky if done secretly. It could escalate the marriage badly if discovered, may not solve the deeper loneliness, and would require careful sexual health precautions. It should not be done impulsively after a fight.
10. The least-bad immediate path suggested was: separate beds, stop initiating sex, stop trying to win her approval, get individual counselling, quietly get family law advice so I understand my options, and focus on being emotionally steady for my son.
11. Relevant Australian supports mentioned: MensLine Australia, Relationships Australia, 1800RESPECT if there is intimidation or family violence, and Lifeline if I start feeling at risk of harming myself.

Overall, I am trying to work out how to stay in the household for my son while emotionally distancing myself from my wife, protecting my mental health, and deciding what to do about the lack of intimacy without coercing her or making an impulsive decision that could make everything worse.
