# Living under the Same Roof
I’m sorry you’re in this position. It is possible to be **separated while still living in the same home**, but it works best if you treat it like a clear, temporary co-parenting and house-sharing arrangement rather than a vague “we’ll see how it goes” situation.

This is general information, not legal advice, but for Victoria/Australia the key point is: your wife does **not** have to agree to the separation, but she does need to know that you consider the relationship over. There is no formal legal process just to become separated. ([Legal Aid Victoria](https://www.legalaid.vic.gov.au/divorce-separation-and-marriage-annulment?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Divorce, separation and marriage annulment"))

## 1. Make the separation clear and document the date

Tell her, ideally calmly and in writing after the conversation, that you consider the relationship separated from a specific date. Keep it factual, not blaming.

That date matters later. If you later apply for divorce and you lived together during part or all of the 12-month separation period, the Federal Circuit and Family Court says you may need extra evidence showing the marriage had changed, such as separate sleeping arrangements, less shared social activity, separate finances, changed household duties, and telling others you are separated. ([Federal Circuit Court](https://www.fcfcoa.gov.au/fl/pubs/separated-under-one-roof "Separated but living under one roof | Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia"))

A practical record could include:

|Area|What to do|
|---|---|
|Sleeping|Separate bedrooms if possible|
|Money|Separate bank accounts and track who pays what|
|Parenting|Written care roster for your son|
|Household tasks|Divide groceries, cooking, washing and cleaning|
|Social/family life|Stop presenting publicly as a couple|
|Communication|Use text/email for important arrangements|

## 2. Set up an “under one roof” house agreement

Try to agree on house rules that reduce friction. Keep it boring and practical:

- Which room each of you sleeps in.
    
- Which spaces are shared and when.
    
- Who buys groceries, nappies, formula, clothes and household basics.
    
- Who cooks for your son and when.
    
- Quiet hours.
    
- Whether either of you can have visitors or new partners at the home. With a toddler, I would strongly suggest no new partners at home during this phase.
    
- A rule that arguments do not happen in front of your son.
    
- A rule that neither parent uses your son as a messenger.
    

Think of the home as two adults sharing a property while co-parenting, not as a couple trying to function with unresolved expectations.

## 3. Build a parenting routine around your son’s stability

Your son is still very young, so the priority is predictable routines: sleep, meals, childcare, bath time, comfort and consistent caregiving. Australian family law focuses on the child’s best interests, and separated parents are encouraged to consult each other on major long-term issues where it is safe to do so. ([Family Relationships Online](https://www.familyrelationships.gov.au/parenting/children-family-law "Children and family law | Family Relationships Online"))

A same-house roster can work like this:

|Time|Parent A|Parent B|
|---|---|---|
|Morning routine|Mon, Wed, Fri|Tue, Thu|
|Childcare drop-off|Parent working nearest route|Backup parent if sick/work conflict|
|Dinner/bath/bed|Alternating nights|Alternating nights|
|Weekends|Split blocks, not vague “shared” time|Split blocks, not vague “shared” time|
|Night wakes|Alternate nights, or divide by work schedule|Alternate nights, or divide by work schedule|

Even though you are in the same house, define who is “on duty”. Otherwise both parents feel constantly responsible, or one parent feels they are doing everything.

## 4. Decide how you’ll record parenting arrangements

If you both agree, you can write a **parenting plan**. It is a written agreement about parenting arrangements, but it is not legally enforceable like a court order. If you want something legally binding, you can apply for **consent orders**. The Court says parents who agree do not need to go to court, but should formally document the agreement to avoid disputes. ([Federal Circuit Court](https://www.fcfcoa.gov.au/fl/children/agree "Children: We have agreed | Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia"))

For now, a simple parenting plan could cover:

- where your son lives while you are under one roof;
    
- who handles mornings, nights, childcare, medical appointments and expenses;
    
- how decisions are made about health, childcare, religion, travel and family visits;
    
- what happens if one of you later moves out;
    
- how you communicate, for example a shared calendar plus text only for logistics.
    

If you cannot agree, Family Dispute Resolution is usually the next step before court, except in urgent or unsafe situations. The Court describes applying for parenting orders by initiating application as a last resort after genuine efforts to resolve the matter. ([Federal Circuit Court](https://www.fcfcoa.gov.au/fl/hdi/apply-parenting-orders "How do I apply for parenting orders? | Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia")) Relationships Australia Victoria also offers family dispute resolution for parenting, child support, property and financial arrangements. ([Relationships Australia Victoria](https://www.relationshipsvictoria.org.au/family-dispute-resolution/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Family dispute resolution (family mediation)"))

## 5. Do not ignore the mortgage, but do not make informal assumptions either

Because you have a mortgage, get legal and financial advice before making big moves like stopping payments, moving out permanently, selling, refinancing, or agreeing that one person “keeps the house”.

A divorce itself does **not** sort out property or child arrangements. Property settlement is separate, and if you divorce before sorting property, there is generally a 12-month deadline after the divorce order to start court proceedings for property settlement if needed. ([Legal Aid Victoria](https://www.legalaid.vic.gov.au/divorce "Divorce | Victoria Legal Aid"))

For the house and mortgage, start gathering:

- mortgage statements;
    
- offset/redraw balances;
    
- council rates, insurance and strata/body corporate fees;
    
- bank and credit card statements;
    
- superannuation balances;
    
- car loan or personal loan details;
    
- income records;
    
- childcare costs and child-related expenses.
    

Victoria Legal Aid says property can be divided by agreement, dispute resolution or court, and it is sensible to get legal help before negotiating and before signing anything. ([Legal Aid Victoria](https://www.legalaid.vic.gov.au/dividing-your-property?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Dividing your property")) If you agree on property, you can consider consent orders, a financial agreement or an informal agreement, but property includes both assets and debts, and financial disclosure is a legal requirement. ([Legal Aid Victoria](https://www.legalaid.vic.gov.au/agreement-about-dividing-property?utm_source=chatgpt.com "If you agree on dividing property"))

## 6. Separate the money in a controlled way

A practical middle-ground is:

1. Keep mortgage payments going if you can.
    
2. Open separate personal accounts if you do not already have them.
    
3. Create a shared spreadsheet for mortgage, utilities, childcare, groceries and your son’s expenses.
    
4. Agree what each person contributes each pay cycle.
    
5. Avoid draining joint accounts or taking on new joint debt.
    
6. Change passwords on personal email, banking, cloud storage and important accounts.
    
7. Keep copies of documents somewhere secure.
    

MoneySmart has a divorce and separation financial checklist covering documents, bills, passwords and practical financial steps after a break-up. ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/family-and-relationships/divorce-and-separation-financial-checklist?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Divorce and separation financial checklist"))

## 7. Check Services Australia and child support implications

If either of you receives Centrelink or family assistance, Services Australia may need to assess whether you are separated under one roof. They have a specific “Separated under one roof” form, and generally both ex-partners complete one unless doing so creates risk. ([Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/ss293 "Relationship details - Separated under one roof form (SS293) - Services Australia"))

For child support, Services Australia usually works out care percentage based on the care each parent provides, and parents may agree how much care each provides. If you cannot agree, they may work it out and may ask for evidence. ([Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/how-your-percentage-care-affects-your-child-support-payments?context=21911&utm_source=chatgpt.com "How your percentage of care affects your child support ..."))

## 8. Protect your son from the emotional fallout

The most important parenting rule is that your son should not be made responsible for adult emotions. That means:

- no arguing in front of him;
    
- no criticising the other parent to him;
    
- no using him to pass messages;
    
- no competing for affection;
    
- no sudden unexplained changes to his routine;
    
- no pretending everything is normal if the atmosphere is tense.
    

For a toddler, the explanation can stay simple: “Mum and Dad are both looking after you. We both love you. Some things at home will be a bit different.”

## 9. Have a safety threshold

If there is any family violence, coercive control, threats, intimidation, stalking, financial abuse, or you think separation will escalate things, living together may not be safe. In immediate danger, call **000**. 1800RESPECT says separation can require safety planning and provides support for people impacted by domestic, family or sexual violence. ([1800RESPECT](https://1800respect.org.au/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Home | 1800RESPECT")) If you are feeling overwhelmed or in crisis, Lifeline is available on **13 11 14**, SMS **0477 13 11 14**, or web chat.

## A realistic next step

Start with three documents: a written separation date, a temporary parenting roster, and a mortgage/household expenses spreadsheet. Then book family law advice before signing anything about the house, parenting, child support or property settlement.
